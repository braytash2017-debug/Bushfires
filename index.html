01

The Nature of Australian Bushfires

Bushfires are a natural part of the Australian landscape. Many native plants, like eucalyptus trees, have adapted to fire and even need heat to open their seeds and grow back.

However, rising global temperatures, prolonged dry spells and heavy fuel accumulation have intensified fire behavior. Ultimately, these changes are turning natural cycles into severe environmental and community threats.

02

Primary Causes & Triggers

Understanding how bushfires ignite is critical to prevention and early response, generally falling into two main categories:

Natural Ignition

Lightning strikes during dry thunderstorms are the primary natural ignition source, often sparking fires in remote or inaccessible bushland.

Human Activity

Accidental sparks from machinery, powerlines, discarded cigarettes, campfires or illegal arson cause major fire outbreaks.

03

Key Factors Influencing Spread

Once a fire starts, three main environmental variables dictate how fast and aggressively it moves across the terrain:

Weather

High ambient temperatures, low humidity, and strong winds rapidly dry out vegetation and drive flames forward.

Fuel Load

The density of dry leaves, bark, fallen branches, and undergrowth provides combustible material for the fire front.

Topography

Fires travel significantly faster uphill. Radiant heat pre-heats the fuel slope ahead of the fire line, accelerating ignition.

04

Safety & Preparation Checklist

Being prepared drastically increases survivability. Below are core actions recommended by emergency services:

  • ✓ Clear gutters and dry leaves within 20 meters of buildings.
  • ✓ Establish a written, practiced Bushfire Survival Plan.
  • ✓ Ensure an independent water supply.
  • ✓ Prepare an emergency kit with radio, wool blankets and documents.
